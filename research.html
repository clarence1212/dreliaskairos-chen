Brain-inspired AI

Most AI today is loosely modelled on the brain. Brain-inspired AI goes further, borrowing how real neurons communicate, learn and adapt over time.

Why it matters for leaders: it points to AI that learns continuously from less data and uses far less energy.

Spiking neural networks

Instead of passing numbers continuously, spiking networks communicate through brief electrical pulses, as biological neurons do. Timing carries information.

Why it matters: they are well suited to signals that change over time, such as sensors, speech, physiological data and markets.

Neuromorphic computing

Hardware and software designed around the brain's architecture, processing information where it is stored rather than shuttling it back and forth.

Why it matters: it could make capable AI run on devices at the edge, with a fraction of today's energy cost.

Intelligent systems

How many specialised AI components, agents and people combine into systems that perceive, decide and act, and where those systems fail.

Why it matters: enterprise AI is becoming a system of agents, so governing the system matters as much as any single model.
